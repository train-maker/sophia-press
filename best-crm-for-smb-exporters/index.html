Best CRM for SMB Exporters 2026 — HubSpot, Pipedrive, Close, Salesforce.

Updated 2026-05-02 · 9-minute read · written by Sophia Press

Disclosure. Some links below are affiliate links — we may earn a commission if you sign up through them. We never accept payment to rank a CRM higher than another. The ranking below is honest editorial.

An SMB exporter doesn't need a CRM with 400 features. They need four things: a place to track buyer inquiries from directories, a place to write follow-up sequences, a way to forecast revenue from the open pipeline, and a way to bill the deals that close. Most CRMs ship with 50x more than that — and charge accordingly. We tested four CRMs from the SMB exporter angle and ranked them by what an actual operator gets for the money.

1. HubSpot — the right starting CRM for almost every SMB exporter

Best for: SMB exporters with 1-10 sellers who want a real free tier and a clean upgrade path.

HubSpot is the only CRM in this review with a meaningful free tier — not a 14-day trial, but a permanent free plan with contact management, deal pipeline, email tracking, and basic reporting for unlimited users. For SMB exporters with limited budget, that's the structural advantage.

What works: the free tier handles the first 100-1,000 contacts well. UX is the cleanest in the review. Email tracking and the meeting scheduler are strong. The Sales Hub Starter ($15/seat/mo) unlocks email sequences, which is the single feature SMB exporters most often need first.

What doesn't: HubSpot's pricing escalates fast as you add Marketing Hub, Service Hub, or Operations Hub. Free is great; "scaled" plans run $1,000+/month if you don't watch them.

2. Pipedrive — pure pipeline focus, no marketing bloat

Best for: SMB exporters whose CRM job is 90% pipeline tracking, not marketing automation.

Pipedrive built its product around the pipeline — drag-and-drop deal stages, forecasting, activity tracking. It doesn't pretend to be a marketing platform. For an exporter whose buyer flow is "directory inquiry → discovery call → quote → close," Pipedrive's mental model is exactly right.

What works: the cheapest entry tier in this review at $14/seat/mo. Pipeline UX is the cleanest of any CRM, anywhere. Email integration is solid.

What doesn't: no real free tier. You start paying immediately after the 14-day trial. Reporting is thinner than HubSpot's.

Try Pipedrive →

3. Close — outbound-heavy teams, built-in calling

Best for: SMB exporters running outbound calling at volume, especially to international buyers.

Close is built for outbound teams. The platform's calling features, email sequencing, and SMS capabilities are tighter than HubSpot's. If your business is "we call 50 international buyers a day and need every one tracked," Close's UX is built for that motion.

What works: the cleanest outbound calling integration. Power Dialer for sequences. Easy SMS to international buyers in markets where phone is the primary channel.

What doesn't: $49-149/seat/mo is the most expensive entry tier in this review. Overkill for SMB exporters whose flow is mostly inbound from directory listings.

4. Salesforce Starter — for teams that will grow into mid-market

Best for: SMB exporters explicitly planning to scale into a 25-100 person sales team within 18 months.

Salesforce's Starter tier ($25/seat/mo) gives you the basic CRM motion. The argument for starting here is "we'll need full Salesforce eventually, so let's not migrate later." That's a real argument for some teams, but most SMB exporters never grow into needing full Salesforce — and migrating from HubSpot to Salesforce when the time comes is a one-week project, not the year-long migration the Salesforce sales team makes it sound like.

What works: clear upgrade path into the Salesforce ecosystem. AppExchange integrations.

What doesn't: UX is meaningfully worse than HubSpot or Pipedrive at the entry tier. Pricing escalates aggressively. For most SMB exporters, this is over-engineering.

What an SMB exporter actually needs from a CRM

Strip away every "AI-powered" feature pitch. The four real jobs:

  1. Capture every inbound inquiry from your directory listings (Sophia, Alibaba, ThomasNet, etc.) into one timeline so nothing slips.
  2. Run a follow-up cadence when a buyer goes silent — three touches over 14 days, then closed.
  3. Forecast the open pipeline — what's in flight, what's likely to close this month, what's stuck.
  4. Hand off to billing when a deal closes — Stripe link, invoice, paper trail.

HubSpot Free + Stripe handles all four for $0 in a month. Pipedrive Essential at $14/seat/mo + Stripe handles them with cleaner pipeline UX. Close handles them with the best outbound flow but at 3-7x the cost.

The integration layer matters more than the CRM itself

The CRM is a contact database. What makes it useful is what it talks to:

  • Email + calendar: all four CRMs handle Gmail, Outlook, iCloud calendar.
  • Stripe: HubSpot has the cleanest native Stripe integration. Pipedrive uses Zapier. Close has its own.
  • WhatsApp: for international SMB sales, this is increasingly the channel. HubSpot's WhatsApp integration is solid, Close's is solid, Pipedrive's needs Zapier.
  • Translation: none of the CRMs do this natively. Use Sophia's listing translation upstream, then route translated inquiries into your CRM in your preferred language.

Final pick

For SMB exporters reading this who haven't picked yet:

Start free on HubSpot. The free tier covers the first six months for almost every SMB. When you outgrow it (usually around 5-10 active sellers), upgrade to Sales Hub Starter at $15/seat/mo. If pipeline-purity is what you want and you don't need a free tier, Pipedrive is the cleaner UX. Skip Close unless you're outbound-heavy. Skip Salesforce unless you're explicitly building a 25+ person sales org.
