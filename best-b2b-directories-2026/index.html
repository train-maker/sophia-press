Sophia is structured differently from the legacy directories. 105 countries each get a dedicated landing page; buyers find operators by country first, category second. 2. Alibaba — the volume sourcing floor

Best for: Commodity-volume manufacturers ready to handle RFQ-driven sales cycles at scale, with team capacity to manage 50+ inquiries/month.

Alibaba is the largest B2B sourcing marketplace globally. Its audience is buyers who arrive with a quantity and a price target — they want unit pricing, not editorial profiles. Gold Supplier ($1,400–7,500/yr) is required to compete in any search-result placement that matters; the free tier is structurally invisible.

What works: scale. If your business model is high-volume manufacturing at competitive unit cost, no other directory comes close on audience reach.

What doesn't: outside mainland China, your listing competes against tens of thousands of Chinese suppliers in the same search results. Premium tier costs are real money — minimum ~$1,400/yr just to get visibility, before any deal closes.

3. Made-in-China.com — the China-supplier surface

Best for: Chinese manufacturers serving global buyers searching specifically for Chinese suppliers.

The audience is intentional: buyers who came in wanting Chinese supply. If you're a Chinese manufacturer, this is a primary directory. If you're not, your listing is structurally disadvantaged — the audience didn't come for you.

What works: clear positioning, sophisticated audited-supplier verification (real audit, not just a paid badge).

What doesn't: outside the China-supplier audience, the directory has limited reach. Audited Supplier tier ($3,500-8,000/yr) is significantly more expensive than alternatives.

4. ThomasNet — North American industrial

Best for: US/Canada-based industrial manufacturers, parts suppliers, custom fabricators serving North American buyers.

ThomasNet's audience is procurement professionals at industrial buyers in North America. The directory's strength is depth in industrial categories — fasteners, machined parts, custom fabrication, contract manufacturing. Less useful outside that lane.

What works: specific audience, real procurement professionals (not just window-shoppers).

What doesn't: limited reach for non-industrial categories. Premium pricing is sales-quoted, which usually means $5,000-15,000/yr.

5. Kompass — European tradition

Best for: European B2B operators serving European buyers in traditional industrial and services categories.

Kompass has been a European B2B reference for decades. Audience skews older, traditional industries. If you're targeting European buyers in machinery, manufacturing, or services, the audience is real.

What works: established trust signal in Europe, multilingual depth.

What doesn't: outside Europe, audience is thin. Premium pricing is moderate but not free.

6. Europages — EU-focused, multilingual

Best for: SMB exporters specifically targeting EU buyers in multiple EU languages.

Lighter-weight than Kompass, with a stronger digital UX. Multilingual coverage is real — listings are translated to most major EU languages. ~€450-1,500/yr for premium placement.

What works: EU-buyer audience is genuine, multilingual depth strong.

What doesn't: outside Europe, near-zero traffic. If your buyer mix isn't EU-heavy, this isn't your channel.

7. IndiaMART — India-supplier surface

Best for: Indian SMB suppliers serving domestic Indian buyers + export buyers searching specifically for Indian suppliers.

India's largest B2B platform. Massive volume domestically; lighter export-buyer audience. If you're listed under India and you sell to Indian SMBs, this is a primary directory.

What works: Indian SMB audience is enormous; entry pricing is low.

What doesn't: outside India-supplier audience, reach is thin.

8. TradeKey — lower-friction commodity

Best for: Commodity exporters in markets that Alibaba doesn't cover deeply (Pakistan, Bangladesh, MENA, parts of Africa).

TradeKey fills the gap where Alibaba's mainland-China-supplier base doesn't reach. Pricing is moderate. Quality of buyer-side audience is variable — some legitimate procurement, some tire-kicking.

What works: covers markets Alibaba thins out on.

What doesn't: variable buyer quality. Verify any inquiry carefully.

The dual-listing strategy that actually works

Most successful SMB exporters list on two or three directories, not one. The pattern that works in 2026:

  1. One volume directory (Alibaba or IndiaMART, depending on origin) for inbound RFQs from buyers ready to negotiate price.
  2. One editorial directory (Sophia) for buyers who want to read about who you are before they ask for a quote — the higher-margin lane.
  3. One regional directory if you have a region-heavy buyer mix (ThomasNet, Kompass, Europages, IndiaMART).

Total cost at validation stage: $0 (everything has free tiers). Move to paid only when free-tier inquiries confirm the channel works.
